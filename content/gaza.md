# Gaza / Israel-Palestine

## Historical Context

The modern conflict traces to the 1947 UN partition plan and the 1948 war surrounding Israel's founding, in which roughly 700,000 Palestinians were displaced ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/11/27/palestine-and-israel-brief-history-maps-and-charts)). Israel occupied the Gaza Strip and West Bank in the 1967 war. The 1993–95 Oslo Accords between Israel and the PLO established limited Palestinian self-government and a planned path toward further negotiations, but the process broke down amid the assassination of Israeli PM Yitzhak Rabin, continued settlement expansion, and the Second Intifada ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Oslo-Accords)). Israel withdrew settlers and troops from Gaza in 2005; Hamas won 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and, after a violent 2007 split with the rival Fatah party, took full control of Gaza, which Israel and Egypt then subjected to a longstanding blockade. Gaza and Israel fought several rounds of war (2008–09, 2012, 2014, 2021) before Hamas and allied militants launched a large-scale assault on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, killing roughly 1,200 people and taking 251 hostages ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/October-7-attack)). Israel responded with a major air and ground campaign in Gaza that continued, through several partial truces, for over two years, alongside a sharp escalation of Israeli settlement activity and settler violence in the occupied West Bank.

## Current State of Facts

A US-brokered ceasefire, built around President Trump's 20-point plan, took effect on 10 October 2025 after talks in Sharm el-Sheikh ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/9/trump-announces-gaza-ceasefire-deal-what-we-know-and-whats-next)). All 251 hostages taken on 7 October 2023 have been accounted for, the last body recovered on 26 January 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/last-hostage-body-returned-gaza-israel-ran-gvili-ceasefire-rafah-trump-rcna241533)). The Gaza Health Ministry's cumulative death toll from the war stood at 74,382 as of 12 October 2026, up from the 73,000-plus recorded in early August 2026, and the ministry reports 1,493 people killed and 5,299 wounded since the ceasefire began (revised upward from the roughly 1,250 post-ceasefire deaths reported in early August) ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/palestine-weekly-gaza-marks-year-of-ceasefire-with-more-israeli-killing)); a joint statement by more than 100 NGOs cites slightly different cut-off figures — at least 1,471 killed since the ceasefire and 74,254 in total ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/9/gaza-ceasefire-exists-in-name-only-more-than-100-ngos-say)) — so the differences reflect reporting dates rather than a disagreement. Independent data-verification efforts have found the ministry's count broadly credible even as exact figures are disputed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/2/18/gaza-death-toll-exceeds-75000-as-independent-data-verify-loss)), and separate independent statistical modeling published in The Lancet Global Health estimates a higher total ([The Lancet Global Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(25)00522-4/fulltext)). The IPC confirmed famine conditions in Gaza Governorate in August 2025; food security has since improved but roughly 1.6 million people remained in Crisis-level or worse food insecurity as of early 2026 ([IPC](https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1159820/), [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/12/1166638)). As of October 2026, Israeli forces control roughly 63–65% of Gaza's territory, up from about 53% when the ceasefire began, per differing estimates ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/one-year-into-ceasefire-vexed-gazans-say-theyre-still-living-like-war-never-ended/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/palestine-weekly-gaza-marks-year-of-ceasefire-with-more-israeli-killing)); the 64% figure cited in May 2026 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/14/middleeast/gaza-israel-ceasefire-permanent-division-intl)) is therefore consistent with that range. In July–August 2026, Trump's "Board of Peace" announced a Hamas disarmament agreement, but Hamas says disarmament is contingent on a full Israeli withdrawal, which the Board says will not happen until disarmament is complete ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/31/g-s1-136500/trump-hamas-gaza)). As of late October 2026 Hamas has not disarmed, large-scale reconstruction has not begun, and the technocratic Palestinian committee formed in January 2026 has not entered Gaza; Hamas announced in July the dissolution of its governmental emergency committee ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/one-year-into-ceasefire-vexed-gazans-say-theyre-still-living-like-war-never-ended/)). On the first anniversary of the ceasefire (10 October 2026), more than 100 aid and rights organizations said the ceasefire "exists in name only", citing near-daily Israeli strikes, aid arriving at roughly a third of the agreed rate, and more than 18,500 patients awaiting medical evacuation ([Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/10/gaza-ceasefire-exists-in-name-only-say-more-than-100-organizations/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/palestine-weekly-gaza-marks-year-of-ceasefire-with-more-israeli-killing)). Separately, the UN and rights groups report record levels of settler violence and Israeli settlement expansion in the West Bank ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1168045)).

## Contested Narratives / Common Myths

**Framing of the war's origin and character.** Israeli officials and many Western governments frame the campaign as self-defense against a designated terrorist organization following the 7 October attack, which Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have separately concluded involved crimes against humanity and war crimes by Hamas-led groups ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/07/17/october-7-crimes-against-humanity-war-crimes-hamas-led-groups); [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/mde15/0282/2025/en/)). Palestinian officials, many international law scholars, and rights groups instead emphasize the scale of Palestinian civilian death and destruction and argue Israel's response was disproportionate; some, including Amnesty International, have used the term "genocide," a characterization Israel's government rejects entirely.

**Casualty figures.** The Gaza Health Ministry — run by the Hamas-controlled administration — is the primary source for Palestinian death tolls; Israeli officials and pro-Israel commentary outlets such as HonestReporting have repeatedly challenged the ministry's methodology and its breakdown of civilian versus combatant deaths, and have pointed to Reuters reporting that the ministry lost track of some individual case records. UN agencies and independent academic modeling (e.g., The Lancet Global Health) have generally found the ministry's toll to be a plausible, likely conservative, baseline, with total mortality (including indirect deaths) potentially higher.

**Territorial control and "who broke the ceasefire."** Israel describes its continued military presence behind the yellow line as a security necessity pending Hamas disarmament; Hamas and Palestinian officials describe it as a unilateral land grab that violates the ceasefire's spirit. Each side has separately accused the other of stalling implementation of the Trump plan's later phases. As of October 2026, the Israeli government says its strikes counter threats to troops and Hamas's rebuilding of its military wing, that troops will not pull back until Hamas disarms, and that a Board of Peace disarmament plan Hamas tentatively accepted does not go far enough; Israeli security officials say Hamas is recruiting and training again. Hamas rejects some of these characterizations (for example, it denies that a civil-defence worker killed near Kamal Adwan Hospital was a sniper, as the Israeli military said), and the aid and rights groups cited above describe the Israeli strikes as a continuing violation of the ceasefire; the Israeli military has not published figures on operatives killed since the truce, so individual targeting claims remain unverified ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/one-year-into-ceasefire-vexed-gazans-say-theyre-still-living-like-war-never-ended/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/palestine-weekly-gaza-marks-year-of-ceasefire-with-more-israeli-killing)).
