# Gaza / Israel-Palestine

## Historical Context

The modern conflict traces to the 1947 UN partition plan and the 1948 war surrounding Israel's founding, in which roughly 700,000 Palestinians were displaced ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/11/27/palestine-and-israel-brief-history-maps-and-charts)). Israel occupied the Gaza Strip and West Bank in the 1967 war. The 1993–95 Oslo Accords between Israel and the PLO established limited Palestinian self-government and a planned path toward further negotiations, but the process broke down amid the assassination of Israeli PM Yitzhak Rabin, continued settlement expansion, and the Second Intifada ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Oslo-Accords)). Israel withdrew settlers and troops from Gaza in 2005; Hamas won 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and, after a violent 2007 split with the rival Fatah party, took full control of Gaza, which Israel and Egypt then subjected to a longstanding blockade. Gaza and Israel fought several rounds of war (2008–09, 2012, 2014, 2021) before Hamas and allied militants launched a large-scale assault on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, killing roughly 1,200 people and taking 251 hostages ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/October-7-attack)). Israel responded with a major air and ground campaign in Gaza that continued, through several partial truces, for over two years, alongside a sharp escalation of Israeli settlement activity and settler violence in the occupied West Bank.

## Current State of Facts

A US-brokered ceasefire, built around President Trump's 20-point plan, took effect on 10 October 2025 after talks in Sharm el-Sheikh ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/9/trump-announces-gaza-ceasefire-deal-what-we-know-and-whats-next)). All 251 hostages taken on 7 October 2023 have been accounted for, the last body recovered on 26 January 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/last-hostage-body-returned-gaza-israel-ran-gvili-ceasefire-rafah-trump-rcna241533)). The Gaza Health Ministry's cumulative death toll from the war had surpassed 73,000 by early August 2026, including roughly 1,250 people killed since the ceasefire began; independent data-verification efforts have found the ministry's count broadly credible even as exact figures are disputed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/2/18/gaza-death-toll-exceeds-75000-as-independent-data-verify-loss)), and separate independent statistical modeling published in The Lancet Global Health estimates a higher total ([The Lancet Global Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(25)00522-4/fulltext)). The IPC confirmed famine conditions in Gaza Governorate in August 2025; food security has since improved but roughly 1.6 million people remained in Crisis-level or worse food insecurity as of early 2026 ([IPC](https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1159820/), [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/12/1166638)). Israeli forces continue to control roughly 64% of Gaza's territory behind a demarcated "yellow/orange line," per maps shared with aid groups ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/14/middleeast/gaza-israel-ceasefire-permanent-division-intl)). In July–August 2026, Trump's "Board of Peace" announced a Hamas disarmament agreement, but Hamas says disarmament is contingent on a full Israeli withdrawal, which the Board says will not happen until disarmament is complete — a standoff still unresolved as of this writing ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/31/g-s1-136500/trump-hamas-gaza)). Separately, the UN and rights groups report record levels of settler violence and Israeli settlement expansion in the West Bank ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1168045)).

## Contested Narratives / Common Myths

**Framing of the war's origin and character.** Israeli officials and many Western governments frame the campaign as self-defense against a designated terrorist organization following the 7 October attack, which Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have separately concluded involved crimes against humanity and war crimes by Hamas-led groups ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/07/17/october-7-crimes-against-humanity-war-crimes-hamas-led-groups); [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/mde15/0282/2025/en/)). Palestinian officials, many international law scholars, and rights groups instead emphasize the scale of Palestinian civilian death and destruction and argue Israel's response was disproportionate; some, including Amnesty International, have used the term "genocide," a characterization Israel's government rejects entirely.

**Casualty figures.** The Gaza Health Ministry — run by the Hamas-controlled administration — is the primary source for Palestinian death tolls; Israeli officials and pro-Israel commentary outlets such as HonestReporting have repeatedly challenged the ministry's methodology and its breakdown of civilian versus combatant deaths, and have pointed to Reuters reporting that the ministry lost track of some individual case records. UN agencies and independent academic modeling (e.g., The Lancet Global Health) have generally found the ministry's toll to be a plausible, likely conservative, baseline, with total mortality (including indirect deaths) potentially higher.

**Territorial control and "who broke the ceasefire."** Israel describes its continued military presence behind the yellow line as a security necessity pending Hamas disarmament; Hamas and Palestinian officials describe it as a unilateral land grab that violates the ceasefire's spirit. Each side has separately accused the other of stalling implementation of the Trump plan's later phases.
