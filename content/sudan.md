# Sudan Civil War

## Historical Context

Sudan's current war grows out of a long history of center-periphery conflict and militia politics dating to the Darfur war that erupted in 2003, when Arab "Janjaweed" militias were armed by President Omar al-Bashir's government to crush a rebellion by non-Arab groups including the Fur, Zaghawa and Masalit — a campaign the International Criminal Court later linked to genocide charges against Bashir ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rapid-Support-Forces)). In 2013, Bashir formalized the Janjaweed into the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), a paramilitary force under his nominal control that he used to counterbalance the regular army. After Bashir's overthrow in 2019 amid mass protests, the army (Sudanese Armed Forces, SAF) and the RSF, led by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and RSF commander Mohamed Hamdan "Hemedti" Dagalo respectively, jointly took power and then jointly suppressed a transition to civilian rule in a 2021 coup. Disputes over integrating the RSF into the regular army — central to internationally backed transition plans — broke into open war between the two former allies on 15 April 2023 ([International Crisis Group](https://www.crisisgroup.org/stm/africa/sudan/after-el-fasher-ending-sudans-atrocious-war)). The war has since split the country geographically, with the SAF holding much of the east and north and the RSF dominant across Darfur and parts of Kordofan, and has drawn in regional powers on both sides.

## Current State of Facts

The RSF captured El Fasher, the capital of North Darfur and the army's last major stronghold in the region, in late October 2025 after an 18-month siege. A UN fact-finding mission and the UN human rights office found that RSF forces committed ethnically targeted killings, mass sexual violence and enforced disappearances during and after the takeover, which it said bore the "hallmarks of genocide" against Zaghawa and Fur communities, and separately assessed RSF violations in the offensive as amounting to war crimes ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/02/1166997); [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/02/sudan-rsf-violations-capture-el-fasher-amount-war-crimes)). The RSF has not substantively rebutted the findings. Fighting has since shifted toward North Kordofan, where the RSF has besieged El-Obeid, prompting UN warnings of an impending humanitarian catastrophe there ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/6/el-obeid-under-siege-by-rsf-could-this-be-sudans-next-el-fasher)). Death toll estimates vary widely by methodology: US officials have cited figures as low as 15,000 and as high as 150,000, while a survey-based academic study by researchers including the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine found more than 60,000 deaths in the Khartoum region alone during the war's first 14 months, suggesting the true nationwide toll is substantially higher than commonly cited figures ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/sudans-civil-war-has-left-at-least-62-000-dead-by-our-estimate-but-the-true-figure-could-be-far-higher-242073)). The UN describes Sudan as the world's largest displacement and hunger crisis, with more than 30 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and famine conditions confirmed in parts of the country.

## Contested Narratives / Common Myths

**Foreign backing of the RSF.** Sudan's army and numerous investigators, including a UN Panel of Experts, have documented evidence — including intercepted weapons shipments routed through Chad and Ethiopia and Chinese-made munitions used by RSF units — pointing to United Arab Emirates military support for the RSF. The UAE has repeatedly and categorically denied arming the RSF, calling the allegations "baseless," and says any weapons identified are commercially available and not exclusive to UAE stockpiles ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/9/uae-denies-supplying-sudan-paramilitaries-with-chinese-arms)).

**Whether the SAF is an "Islamist" force.** The RSF and its political allies frame the war partly as a fight against the return to power of Islamists loyal to the former Bashir regime, noting that Islamist-aligned brigades and remnants of the dissolved National Congress Party have played a significant role reinforcing SAF ranks. Sudanese civilian democracy activists and some Western analysts share concern that the war is enabling an Islamist political comeback within the army's coalition. Critics of the RSF's framing counter that Hemedti's own forces originated as the Bashir-era Janjaweed and were themselves responsible for the Darfur atrocities of the 2000s, calling RSF claims to be fighting for "democracy" against the SAF hypocritical given that record ([FDD](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2025/09/22/a-surge-in-islamism-widens-rift-between-sudanese-army-and-its-regional-allies/)).

**Scale of casualties.** Because access to conflict zones — especially Darfur — is severely restricted, there is no consensus death toll; figures cited by officials and researchers span roughly 15,000 to over 150,000 or more depending on whether indirect deaths from starvation and collapsed healthcare are included, and no single estimate should be treated as definitive.
