# Syria Civil War and Transition

## Historical Context

The Assad family ruled Syria for over 50 years, with Bashar al-Assad succeeding his father Hafez in 2000. Inspired by the wider Arab Spring, peaceful protests began in Deraa in March 2011 after teenagers were arrested and tortured for anti-government graffiti; a harsh military crackdown, compounded by years of drought and economic strain, escalated into a nationwide armed insurgency by late 2011 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Syrian-Civil-War)). The ensuing civil war drew in a patchwork of rebel factions, Kurdish forces, jihadist groups including ISIS and al-Qaeda-linked Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), and foreign powers — Russia and Iran backing Assad, and Turkey, Gulf states, and the US backing various opposition and Kurdish forces — killing hundreds of thousands and displacing millions over more than a decade. In late November 2024, HTS, led by Ahmed al-Sharaa (a former al-Qaeda-affiliated commander), launched a rapid offensive from Idlib, capturing Aleppo, Hama, and Homs within days. Assad fled to Russia on 8 December 2024 as rebels entered Damascus, ending his family's rule ([House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10161/)). HTS declared a transitional government, and al-Sharaa was named interim president in January 2025, later formalizing sweeping executive powers under a March 2025 constitutional declaration for a five-year transition.

## Current State of Facts

Syria's transitional government under President Ahmed al-Sharaa has pursued international rehabilitation: in November 2025 the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 2799, removing al-Sharaa and Interior Minister Anas Khattab, along with HTS, from the UN's ISIL/al-Qaeda sanctions list, and the US and other governments have lifted most remaining sanctions ([Security Council Report](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/monthly-forecast/2026-06/syria-92.php); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/11/syrians-optimistic-but-cautious-as-sanctions-removal-revives-economic-hopes)). Sectarian violence has repeatedly tested the transition. In March 2025, Amnesty International documented that government-affiliated militias killed more than 100 Alawite civilians in Banias in reprisal attacks following an insurgent ambush of security forces, with Reuters later reporting a wider death toll of roughly 1,500 Alawites killed at some 40 sites along the coast ([Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/04/syria-coastal-massacres-of-alawite-civilians-must-be-investigated-as-war-crimes/)). In July 2025, clashes between Druze militias and Bedouin tribes in Sweida province escalated after government forces intervened; Israel launched strikes on Damascus and Sweida, saying it was protecting the Druze, and Human Rights Watch documented grave abuses on multiple sides before a US-, Turkish- and Arab-mediated ceasefire took hold ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/07/22/syria-abuses-humanitarian-emergency-amid-sweida-clashes)). In January 2026, Damascus and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF), after further clashes, reached an agreement for the gradual integration of SDF fighters and civil institutions into the Syrian state, including government forces entering Hasakeh and Qamishli ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/1/30/kurdish-led-sdf-agrees-integration-with-syrian-government-forces)).

## Contested Narratives / Common Myths

**Is al-Sharaa a reformed statesman or a rebranded jihadist?** The transitional government and its Western and Gulf backers present al-Sharaa's break from al-Qaeda and ISIS-linked networks as genuine, pointing to his outreach to minorities, adoption of a constitutional framework, and the removal of terrorism designations by the US, UK, and UN as evidence of a durable shift. Critics — including some Syrian minority communities, Israeli commentators, and analysts at institutions such as Chatham House — argue his consolidation of near-total executive power, the limited representation of Druze and Kurdish areas in the new government, and the participation of hardline factions in state security forces raise doubts about how complete the transformation is ([House of Commons Library, citing Chatham House analysis](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/syria-what-is-the-situation-five-months-after-assads-fall/)).

**Responsibility for the coastal and Sweida massacres.** The transitional government has characterized the March 2025 coastal killings as the actions of undisciplined militias and remnants of Assad loyalists provoking a security response, and has pledged accountability. Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch instead documented a pattern of deliberate, identity-based killings of Alawite civilians by government-aligned forces and called for independent investigation into command responsibility, noting that restricted media access has hampered verification ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2025/09/23/are-you-alawi/identity-based-killings-during-syrias-transition)). In Sweida, Druze leaders and Damascus have offered competing accounts of who initiated the violence and whether government intervention protected or endangered Druze civilians, while Israel frames its strikes as humanitarian protection — a framing Damascus rejects as a pretext for undermining Syrian sovereignty.
